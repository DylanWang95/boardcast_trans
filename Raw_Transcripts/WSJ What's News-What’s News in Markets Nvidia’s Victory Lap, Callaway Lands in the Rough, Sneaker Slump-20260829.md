Before this week, the chip giant's shares had fallen the day after six of its previous eight earnings reports, as investors grew accustomed to quarter after quarter of blockbuster results. This time was different. NVIDIA shares surged 8.7% Thursday, their biggest one-day jump in more than a year, after the company said it expects revenue to grow 70% in its next fiscal year, far above the roughly 45% analysts had been forecasting. CEO Jensen Huang said demand is actually even stronger than that. The biggest thing holding NVIDIA back right now is getting enough chips and components to meet it. Overall, NVIDIA shares ended the week 1.3% higher, lifting the market value of the world's most valuable company to $5.5 trillion. But NVIDIA's victory lap doesn't mean questions about the durability of the AI boom have disappeared. The company is increasingly using its own balance sheet to help customers pay for the massive data center build-out needed to buy its chips. Try not to alienate your fastest-growing customer base. The golf equipment maker found itself apologizing for an ad that showed a male golfer shoving a woman to the ground. Do not touch my new driver. The video was produced by privately held media company Good Good Golf to promote a co-branded Callaway Golf Club. It racked up more than 5 million views on X and plenty of backlash. Both companies eventually apologized, with Callaway's CEO saying the ad's approval, quote, should never have happened. Recently, Callaway posted a strong quarter. But Syracuse University sports analytics professor Justin Ehrlich told our colleagues at MarketWatch that the company's ability to attract new golfers, especially women, quote, may be compromised. Callaway shares fell 2% Tuesday as the controversy unfolded and ended the week about 1.3% lower. And one of the worst performing stocks in the Dow this week was Nike Not because of anything the sneaker maker did but because of something a competitor said Dick's Sporting Goods shares plunged more than 30% Tuesday, the stock's biggest one-day drop on record, after the retailer cut its annual profit outlook. The company's executive chairman, Ed Stack, said the sneaker industry is carrying too much inventory. Brands have been offering bigger discounts to move shoes off the shelves, and those promotions are spilling into the broader market. Stack expects the pressure to continue through the rest of this year. That's bad news for the athletic wear industry, and especially Nike, which had already cut its outlook in June, citing weaker demand and heavier discounting. Nike and Dick's sporting goods fell 2.9% and 26% respectively. That extended into other sports retailers. On Running fell nearly 4% this week, while Under Armour has lost more than 6%.
